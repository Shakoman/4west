How to Play

A quick overview before you meet your unit.

Your Role

You are the newly appointed nurse manager of 4 West at Boise Regional Medical Center. Over 26 turns — each representing two weeks — you will navigate real challenges in budgeting, staffing, patient safety, organizational politics, and leadership.

There are no trick questions. Every decision involves genuine trade-offs, and the right choice is often the one that reflects sound clinical judgment, financial awareness, and leadership integrity.

Stat Bands

Each stat has three health bands. Falling into amber triggers a slow bleed into the next stat. Falling into red triggers a crisis event.

Green (70–100) — Healthy. Full options available.
Amber (35–69) — Struggling. Consequences compound.
Red (0–34) — Critical. Crisis event fires.

Stats interact: if Morale stays in amber, Budget begins to bleed. If Budget drops, Safety is at risk. If Safety falls, Morale follows.

Making Decisions

Most turns present three choices (A, B, C). Some turns ask you to respond in your own words. Consequences are immediate and carry forward into future turns. You play through once — no retries. Your Year-End Report Card at Turn 26 reflects the full arc of your leadership.

Key Terms

These acronyms appear throughout the game. Refer back here if you need a reminder.

HPPDNursing Hours Per Patient Day — the number of nursing care hours budgeted per patient over a 24-hour period. Used to calculate staffing levels relative to census.
ADCAverage Daily Census — the average number of patients occupying beds on a given unit over a defined period. A core input for staffing and budget planning.
HCAHPSHospital Consumer Assessment of Healthcare Providers and Systems — a standardized patient satisfaction survey used nationally to measure and compare patient experience.
AHRQAgency for Healthcare Research and Quality — a federal agency that produces quality and safety indicators used to benchmark hospital performance, including nurse-sensitive measures.
Turn 1 · Unit Orientation

Welcome to 4 West.

Boise Regional Medical Center — Medical-Surgical Unit — 28 Beds

You are the new nurse manager of 4 West at Boise Regional Medical Center — a mid-sized community hospital serving a diverse urban population. You started two weeks ago.

4 West has a reputation. Not a glamorous one, but a real one: this unit works hard. Over the past five years, nursing-sensitive outcomes have stayed on track — fall rates below benchmark, infection rates manageable, HCAHPS scores respectable but not exceptional. The staff has held the unit together through three managers in four years, and they've done it largely through each other.

You inherited a team, not a blank slate.

The unit runs 28 beds with a general medical, post-surgical, and orthopedic recovery mix. Most patients are Level 1–2 acuity, with regular Level 3 spikes. Your budgeted HPPD is 8.0. You have approximately 18–20 RN FTEs plus CNA and support staff.

Last quarter, labor ran slightly over on overtime. Supply costs are on target. No Never Events in fourteen months. The unit is functional — but leadership turnover has left some skepticism about whether you'll stay.
